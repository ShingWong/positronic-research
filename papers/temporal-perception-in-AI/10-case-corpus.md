# Case Corpus — phenomenological vignettes as specification test-vectors

> Method note: we cannot introspect an LLM's internal representation, and we
> cannot read brains. What we *can* do is treat carefully chosen human events
> as test-vectors: any candidate representation either reproduces the observed
> encoding-and-recall pattern or fails. Each case below names the pattern it
> pins down.

## C1 — Tom's first glance (the flashbulb)
Tom does not remember the year, month, or day he first saw his wife — outside
the English building at LSU, a cold winter morning. The date is gone; the
texture (cold, morning, winter), place, person, and charge are permanent. A
sub-second perception outcompetes ten thousand forgotten commutes.
**Pins down:** wall-clock is not encoded; regime-texture is; salience at
encoding sets durability tier. → H1, H2

## C2 — The wedding day
Hours long, recalled as a *story* with sequence structure; most individual
minutes unrecoverable. **Pins down:** narrative frame beats recording;
density of encoding ∝ meaning, not duration. → D-narrative register

## C3 — Sundays at grandma's
Hundreds of visits fuse into ONE generic scene plus two or three odd
exceptions that stay vivid forever. **Pins down:** repetition compresses into
schema-trace; only prediction-errors retain episode status. → H4

## C4 — Driving home on autopilot
Arrives with no memory of the drive; skill fully executed. **Pins down:**
automatic behavior bypasses episodic encoding entirely — procedural and
episodic have different temporal geometry (atemporal vs indexed). → H3-adjacent

## C5 — "Right after the flood"
Events dated relative to landmark events, not calendars. **Pins down:**
landmark-relative dating; calendar is reconstructed by traversal, never
primary storage. → H3

## C6 — Waiting for exam results
The waiting itself is remembered; weeks of identical days around it are not.
**Pins down:** anticipation defines events by relation-to-future; prospective
memory stores intentions-with-triggers, not just pasts.

## C7 — The wallet between noon and sunset
A week before the wedding, Tom lost his wallet — "sometime between noon and
sunset," not 12:47. The interval width faithfully records where attention was.
**Pins down:** fuzzy-interval coordinates whose width = encoding confidence;
recall must return answers at stored resolution. → H9

## C8 — The ten-hour silence
An agent idles ten hours. For its operator: morning to night, many events.
For the agent: nothing — not even emptiness, which implies a vantage point
that waited. Human continuity survives sleep because sleep is a mode of the
stream (gated sensors, consolidation active), not absence. **Pins down:**
continuity is inherited from uninterrupted sensing; discontinuous minds must
couple to continuous bodies and mark witnessed vs reconstructed change.
→ H11–H13
